# Humans as Attack Vectors

## 1. Human Element

Humans are often the weakest link in cybersecurity because attackers can manipulate people instead of directly exploiting technical vulnerabilities.

**Why target humans?**

* Access to accounts, emails, VPNs and databases
* Employees may have privileged access
* Easier to manipulate a person than bypass strong technical controls

## 2. Social Engineering

**Social engineering** = manipulating people using psychological techniques to make them perform an action or reveal information.

Common techniques:

* **Trust** — pretending to be someone legitimate
* **Emotion** — creating urgency, fear or curiosity
* **Impersonation** — pretending to be a CEO, IT staff, bank, etc.

## 3. Common Human Attack Vectors

### Phishing

Fake emails/messages designed to steal credentials or make users perform an unsafe action.

Examples:

* Fake login pages
* Fake security alerts
* Malicious links
* Fake invoices/emails

### Malware Downloads

Attackers trick users into downloading malware through:

* Fake software updates
* Malicious attachments
* Fake CAPTCHA/verification pages
* Malicious websites

### Deepfakes

AI-generated fake audio/video/images used to impersonate trusted people.

### Impersonation

Attacker pretends to be a trusted person such as IT staff, a manager, bank employee or government worker.

## 4. Mitigation

| Control                     | Purpose                                  |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Anti-phishing solution      | Blocks/detects phishing emails           |
| Antivirus / EDR             | Detects and prevents malware             |
| Security awareness training | Teaches employees to identify attacks    |
| Trust but verify            | Verify suspicious requests before acting |

## 5. SOC Analyst Role

A SOC analyst helps defend against human-based attacks by:

* Monitoring security alerts
* Investigating suspicious activity
* Detecting phishing/malware incidents
* Supporting security awareness
* Recommending security improvements

**Key idea:** Mitigation reduces the chance/impact of an attack, while the SOC detects and investigates attacks that get through.

## 7. Key Takeaways

* Humans are a major attack surface.
* Social engineering exploits **people and psychology**.
* Phishing is one of the most common social-engineering attacks.
* Deepfakes and impersonation make verification more important.
* **Training + technical controls + SOC monitoring** provide layered defence.
